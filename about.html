About Data in DP

Data in DP is an independent, data-driven project dedicated to bringing transparency to the U.S. housing market. Our mission is simple: to make complex, fragmented public data accessible, understandable, and actionable for everyday homebuyers, investors, and researchers.

Why We Built This Site

Buying a home is the largest financial decision most people will ever make. Yet, the data needed to make an informed decision is scattered across dozens of government agencies. The Census Bureau holds income data, the FHFA tracks price indices, FEMA maps climate risks, and the EIA tracks energy costs. Trying to piece this puzzle together is nearly impossible for the average buyer.

We built Data in DP to solve this problem. By aggregating 10+ official datasets and processing them through our unified pipeline, we provide a "True Cost of Homeownership" view. We don't just show you the listing price; we show you the property taxes, the insurance burden, the climate risk, and the potential solar ROI—all in one place.

Our Data Philosophy

We believe that public data should be free and accessible. We do not sell leads, we are not a brokerage, and we do not push financial products. Every score we generate—from the Livability Score to the Solar Payback period—is backed by a documented, transparent formula. We openly publish our methodology so that you can verify our numbers and understand the limitations.

Who Runs This Site

Data in DP is maintained by a small team of data analysts and software engineers with a passion for open data. We are not real estate agents or lenders. We are simply data enthusiasts who believe that better information leads to better decisions.

Our Data Sources

All analytics on this site are derived from public datasets. The primary sources include:

  • U.S. Census Bureau (ACS): Demographics, housing values, rent, and income.
  • FHFA: House Price Index (HPI) and market momentum.
  • FRED: Real-time 30-year mortgage rate tracking.
  • FEMA National Risk Index: Climate and natural hazard risk scores.
  • HearthMap: County-level insurance premium estimates.
  • NREL (National Renewable Energy Laboratory): Solar radiation data.
  • U.S. EIA: State and county electricity rates.
  • OpenStreetMap: Amenity density data.

Editorial Principles

  • Transparency: Every composite score has a published formula and weight set.
  • No Advice: We provide data and explanations, not financial or real estate advice.
  • Stated Limits: County averages can mask block-level variation. We document these limitations.
  • Corrections: If you find an error, please contact us immediately so we can fix it.
