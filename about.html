About Data in DP

Data in DP is an independent data project that publishes county-level housing, livability, mortgage, property tax, and insurance risk information for all 3,100+ U.S. counties. Our goal is simple: make public real estate data easier to understand, compare, and use.

Why This Site Exists

Most public housing datasets are published in formats that are difficult for ordinary readers to interpret. A single CSV from the Census Bureau may contain dozens of columns and no explanation of what they mean together. A FEMA hazard index may look precise while leaving out the local context needed to interpret it. Data in DP exists to bridge that gap: we combine official datasets, normalize them into comparable scores, and present them alongside clear explanations of how each score is built and what it does — and does not — tell you.

Readers come to this site for very different reasons. Some want to compare cost of living across counties before relocating. Others want to understand how property taxes affect a monthly payment. Others are checking whether climate risk should change their assumptions about a home they are considering. Our tools are designed to serve all three groups by showing the raw numbers and the reasoning behind them.

Who Runs This Site

Data in DP is maintained by a small independent team with a background in data analysis and public data research. We are not a brokerage, lender, insurer, or government agency. We do not sell real estate or financial products. Our only goal is to help readers make better-informed decisions by making public data more transparent.

If you find an error in our calculations, we want to hear about it. Corrections are published as soon as they are verified. See the Contact page for how to reach us.

Our Data Sources

All analytics on this site are derived from public datasets. The primary sources are:

  • U.S. Census Bureau — American Community Survey (ACS): housing values, rent, income, and owner-occupancy statistics.
  • FHFA House Price Index (HPI): market momentum and price trends.
  • FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data): mortgage rate tracking.
  • HUD: FHA and conforming loan limits.
  • FBI Uniform Crime Reporting: violent and property crime rates by county.
  • FEMA National Risk Index: climate and natural hazard risk scores.
  • CDC PLACES: county-level health indicators.
  • OpenStreetMap: amenity density (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0).
  • HearthMap (NBER Working Paper 32579, CC BY 4.0): county insurance premium estimates.

Full attribution appears in the footer of every page. A complete description of how each score is calculated is available on our Methodology page.

Editorial Principles

  • Transparency: Every composite score has a published formula and weight set. We do not hide how the numbers are built.
  • No advice: We provide data and explanations, not financial, legal, or real estate advice. Readers should consult licensed professionals before making decisions about a specific property.
  • Stated limits: County averages can mask block-level variation, and percentile scores describe relative position rather than absolute quality. We document these limitations in every relevant guide.
  • Corrections: If you find an error in our data or formulas, please contact us so we can correct it and note the change.

What We Do Not Do

We do not collect personal financial information. We do not run mortgage applications or insurance quotes. We do not provide investment recommendations. The calculators on this site are illustrative tools; they are not underwriting decisions and do not constitute an offer of credit.
