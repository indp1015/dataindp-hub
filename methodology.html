Methodology

This page documents how the major scores and metrics on Data in DP are calculated, including formulas, default weights, data sources, and known limitations.

1. Livability Score (Six Axes)

The Livability Score is a composite of six axes. Each axis is first normalized into a 0–100 percentile rank across all U.S. counties. The default weights are shown below.

AxisDefault WeightUnderlying Data
Affordability25%Census ACS — price-to-income ratio
Stability15%FHFA HPI volatility and drawdown history
Momentum15%FHFA HPI recent appreciation
Safety15%FBI UCR — violent and property crime rates
Health10%CDC PLACES — five indicators
Amenities20%OpenStreetMap POI density

Formula: Livability = Σ(axis_i × weight_i) / Σ(weight_i), where each axis is a percentile rank from 0 to 100.

2. Market Score (Four Axes)

The Market Score is designed for investment evaluation and uses four axes: Affordability (30%), Stability (25%), Momentum (25%), and Vitality (20%). Vitality looks at owner-occupancy and employment data. Both Livability and Market scores use the same percentile normalization method.

3. Effective Property Tax Rate

The effective property tax rate is calculated as annual property tax paid ÷ median home value, expressed as a percentage. We derive it from Census ACS 5-Year Estimates at the county level. This is not the same as the statutory (nominal) tax rate, which is applied to assessed value.

4. Insurance and Climate Risk Score

The composite insurance risk score combines three inputs, each normalized into a percentile: FEMA National Risk Index (hazard exposure and expected annual loss), HearthMap county insurance premium estimates, and Housing Affordability (premium burden relative to income).

5. Solar ROI and Payback Period

Our Solar platform calculates the payback period by dividing the net system cost by the annual electricity bill savings. The calculation assumes:

  • Net Cost: Installation cost minus the 30% Federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC).
  • Electricity Rates: Based on EIA 2025 state-level data.
  • Solar Production: Based on NREL NSRDB solar radiation data (10-year average, 2005–2015), adjusted for a standard 0.5% annual panel degradation.
  • Rate Escalation: We assume a 2.5% annual increase in electricity rates.

The payback period is the number of years required for the cumulative savings to equal the net installation cost. The 25-year ROI is calculated by summing the projected savings over 25 years and dividing by the net installation cost.

6. Known Limitations

  • County averages mask local variation. A county score describes the county as a whole; individual neighborhoods may differ substantially.
  • Percentile scores are relative. A score of 90 means "higher than 90% of counties," not "90 out of 100 in absolute quality."
  • Data lags. ACS, FHFA, and EIA data are published with a lag; scores reflect the most recent available release.
  • Solar estimates are not quotes. Actual solar costs depend on roof condition, shading, local installer pricing, and utility net-metering policies.
