Understanding the FEMA National Risk Index

Climate change is reshaping the U.S. housing market. From wildfires in California to flooding in Florida, the physical risks to a home are increasingly translating into financial risks. The FEMA National Risk Index (NRI) is the gold standard for measuring these risks at the county level.

1. What is the FEMA NRI?

The NRI is a dataset published by the Federal Emergency Management Agency that assesses the risk of 18 different natural hazards (including flood, wildfire, earthquake, and hurricane) for every county and census tract in the United States.

2. How the Score is Calculated

FEMA's risk score is not just about how likely a disaster is to happen; it's about the potential consequences. The score combines three factors:

  • Expected Annual Loss (EAL): The average economic loss (to buildings, people, and agriculture) expected each year due to natural hazards.
  • Social Vulnerability: The susceptibility of local populations to the negative impacts of hazards (e.g., low-income households or elderly populations).
  • Community Resilience: The ability of a community to prepare for and recover from a disaster (based on infrastructure, governance, and social systems).

3. How to Use the Risk Score in Your Home Search

When you use our True Cost Dashboard, you will see a FEMA Climate Risk Score (out of 100) for each county. Here is how to interpret it:

  • Very High Risk (80-100): Expect higher insurance premiums, potential difficulty finding private coverage, and a higher likelihood of property damage over a 30-year mortgage.
  • Relatively Moderate (40-60): Typical for many U.S. counties. Check which specific hazards are driving the score (e.g., tornadoes vs. hail).
  • Very Low Risk (0-20): Lower insurance burdens, but you should still verify localized risks (like flash flooding) that the county average might miss.

4. The Bottom Line

A high risk score does not mean you shouldn't buy a home there. It simply means you need to factor insurance costs and resilience measures into your budget and long-term plan. Always pair our data with a property-specific inspection and a flood zone determination from a licensed professional.
