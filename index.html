    Current U.S. Real Estate Market Insights

    Navigating the U.S. real estate market requires understanding the delicate balance between home prices, mortgage rates, and local tax burdens. In today's environment, buyers and investors must look beyond the listing price to grasp the full financial picture — and increasingly, quality-of-life factors and energy costs play a role in where people choose to live.

    1. The Impact of Mortgage Rates on Affordability

    Over the past few years, the 30-year fixed mortgage rate has experienced significant volatility. When rates rise, even a one percentage point increase can add hundreds of dollars to a monthly payment. It is also worth separating nominal affordability from effective affordability. A county with lower home prices but higher property taxes and insurance premiums can end up costing more per month than a higher-priced county with low carrying costs. Our PITI-based tools make these trade-offs visible.

    2. Housing Market Momentum and Stability

    The housing market is not a monolith; it is a collection of thousands of local micro-economies. Our Housing & Livability platform analyzes county-level data to determine market phases alongside a separate Livability Score across six axes. Where the Market Score focuses on investment dynamics, Livability focuses on residence and lifestyle. Adjusting the weights lets you see which counties best fit your own priorities.

    3. The Hidden Cost: Property Taxes and Insurance

    A common oversight for homebuyers is underestimating property taxes and insurance. Effective property tax rates can vary drastically from one state to another. In some regions, high property taxes can significantly erode the return on investment for rental properties. Furthermore, climate risk is increasingly a pricing factor. FEMA's National Risk Index and county-level premium data reveal that two otherwise similar homes can carry very different insurance burdens.

    4. Solar ROI as a Homeownership Cost Offset

    As electricity rates rise, homeowners are increasingly looking at solar energy not just as an environmental choice, but as a financial hedge. Our Solar ROI platform combines EIA electricity rate data with NREL solar radiation data to calculate county-level payback periods. In states with high utility rates, a solar installation can pay for itself in under a decade, effectively offsetting a significant portion of the long-term costs of homeownership.

    5. How to Use This Site Responsibly

    All data on this site is derived from public sources and is intended for education and research. It is not financial, legal, or real estate advice. County averages can mask block-level variation, and percentile scores describe relative position rather than absolute quality. We document these limitations on our methodology page so that readers can judge the data for themselves.

    Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

    What is the Livability Score, and how is it different from the Market Score?

    Livability Score is a six-axis composite built for residence and lifestyle decisions. Market Score is a separate four-axis composite built for investment evaluation. Both use percentile ranking across all U.S. counties. You can adjust Livability weights to match your own priorities on the Livability page.

    Where does the safety, health, and amenity data come from?

    Safety uses FBI Uniform Crime Reporting data. Health uses CDC PLACES estimates across five indicators. Amenities uses OpenStreetMap point-of-interest density. All three are public datasets — full attribution appears in our About section.

    Why do property taxes and insurance premiums vary so much between counties?

    Property taxes are determined by local government budget needs and assessment practices. Insurance premiums reflect local hazard exposure (e.g., flood, wildfire) and the cost of rebuilding. Our property tax guide and climate risk guide explain how to interpret these differences.

    How is the solar payback period calculated?

    We calculate net cost after the 30% federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC), then accumulate annual savings year-by-year (accounting for a 2.5% rate escalation and 0.5% panel degradation). The data relies on NREL solar radiation estimates and EIA electricity rates. Read our solar ROI guide for details.

    Can I use your calculators for investment properties?

    Yes, but note that investment property mortgage rates are typically 0.5% to 1.0% higher than primary residence rates, and property taxes may be reassessed upon purchase depending on state law.

    Why do I see a fixed value before live data loads?

    To keep the page readable for every visitor — including those on slow connections or with JavaScript disabled — we display recent reference values directly in the HTML. When live data feeds are reachable, the JavaScript layer replaces those values with the latest figures.

    About Data in DP & Our Data Sources

    Data in DP was created to democratize access to complex real estate data. We believe that informed decisions are the best decisions — whether you are buying your first home, refinancing a mortgage, or evaluating energy savings across counties.

    All data and analytics across our platforms are sourced from reliable public datasets and official government agencies. Our primary sources include:

    • U.S. Census Bureau (ACS): demographics, housing values, rent, and income.
    • FHFA: House Price Index (HPI) and market momentum data.
    • FRED: real-time mortgage rate tracking.
    • HUD: official FHA loan limits.
    • FBI Uniform Crime Reporting: county-level crime rates.
    • FEMA National Risk Index: climate and natural hazard risk scores.
    • CDC PLACES: county-level health indicators.
    • U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA): state and county electricity rates.
    • NREL (National Renewable Energy Laboratory): solar radiation data (NSRDB).
    • OpenStreetMap: amenity density data.

    We continuously update our pipelines to ensure users are interacting with the most current and reliable data available. For a detailed description of every formula and weight, please see our Methodology page.
