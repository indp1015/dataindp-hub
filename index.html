Current U.S. Real Estate Market Insights

Navigating the U.S. real estate market requires understanding the delicate balance between home prices, mortgage rates, and local tax burdens. In today's dynamic environment, buyers and investors must look beyond the listing price to grasp the full financial picture — and increasingly, quality-of-life factors like safety, health, and amenities.

1. The Impact of Mortgage Rates on Affordability

Over the past few years, the 30-year fixed mortgage rate has experienced significant volatility, heavily influencing buyer purchasing power. When rates rise, even a 1% increase can add hundreds of dollars to a monthly payment. Our Mortgage Tools section is designed to help you navigate this. By utilizing our amortization schedules and FHA/VA loan limit calculators, prospective buyers can accurately model their monthly obligations. Understanding loan limits—especially the differences between conforming loans and FHA loans—is crucial for securing favorable terms in competitive markets.

2. Housing Market Momentum and Stability

The housing market is not a monolith; it is a collection of thousands of local micro-economies. Our Housing & Livability platform analyzes county-level data to determine Market Phases—ranging from "Boom" to "Recovering"—alongside a separate Livability Score across six axes. Where Market Score focuses on investment dynamics (4 axes), Livability focuses on residence and lifestyle (6 axes: affordability, stability, momentum, safety, health, amenities). Adjusting the weights lets you see which counties best fit your priorities.

3. The Hidden Cost: Property Taxes

A common oversight for homebuyers is underestimating property taxes. Effective property tax rates can vary drastically from one state to another, and even from county to county within the same state. In some regions, high property taxes can significantly erode the return on investment for rental properties. Our Property Tax database provides transparency, offering effective tax rates for over 3,000 counties. This allows users to calculate the true, long-term cost of homeownership before making a commitment.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the Livability Score, and how is it different from the Market Score?

Livability Score is a 6-axis composite (affordability, stability, momentum, safety, health, amenities) built for residence and lifestyle decisions. Market Score is a separate 4-axis composite (affordability, stability, momentum, vitality) built for investment evaluation. Both use percentile ranking across all U.S. counties. You can adjust Livability weights to match your own priorities on the Livability page.

How is the "Market Score" calculated on the Housing site?

Our Market Score is a composite metric (0-100) derived from four key pillars: Affordability (30%), Stability (25%), Momentum (25%), and Vitality (20%). We use percentile ranking normalization across 3,100+ U.S. counties. Affordability is based on the price-to-income ratio, Stability tracks historical price volatility, Momentum measures recent appreciation, and Vitality looks at owner-occupancy and employment data.

Where does the safety, health, and amenity data come from?

Safety uses FBI Uniform Crime Reporting data (violent and property crime rates) with calibrated corrections for NYC boroughs and Washington, D.C. Health uses CDC PLACES estimates across five indicators (obesity, diabetes, physical inactivity, high blood pressure, mental health). Amenities uses OpenStreetMap point-of-interest density across six categories (education, healthcare, sustenance, finance, entertainment, grocery). All three are public datasets — full attribution is in our About section.

Are the FHA and VA loan limits on your site accurate for 2026?

Yes. Our Mortgage site uses the official HUD formulas and FHFA data to calculate conforming and FHA loan limits. We use the official formula: FHA = min(1,209,750, max(541,287, 0.65 × conforming)) to ensure accuracy even in areas where raw datasets have missing values.

Why do property taxes vary so much between counties?

Property taxes are primarily determined by local government budget needs (for schools, infrastructure, and services) and the total assessed value of properties in that area. Some states rely heavily on property taxes, while others rely more on sales or income taxes. Our Property Tax site pulls the latest ACS 5-Year Estimates to show you the effective tax rate — the actual percentage of your home's value paid annually.

Can I use your calculators for investment properties?

Absolutely. While our primary focus is on primary residences, our calculators can be used for investment properties. Note that investment property mortgage rates are typically 0.5% to 1.0% higher than primary residence rates, and property taxes may be reassessed upon purchase depending on state law.

About Data in DP & Our Data Sources

Data in DP was created to democratize access to complex real estate data. We believe that informed decisions are the best decisions — whether you are buying your first home, refinancing a mortgage, or comparing quality of life across counties.

All data and analytics across our platforms are sourced from reliable public datasets and official government agencies. Our primary sources include:

  • U.S. Census Bureau (ACS): Demographics, housing values, rent, and owner-occupancy statistics.
  • FHFA (Federal Housing Finance Agency): House Price Index (HPI) and market momentum data.
  • FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data): Real-time mortgage rate tracking.
  • HUD (Housing and Urban Development): Official FHA loan limits.
  • FBI Uniform Crime Reporting: County-level violent and property crime rates.
  • CDC PLACES: County-level health indicators (public domain / ODC-ODbL 1.0; not endorsed by CDC or HHS).
  • OpenStreetMap: Amenity density data (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0).

We continuously update our pipelines to ensure users are interacting with the most current and reliable data available. Our goal is a seamless, integrated experience — from market analysis to financial calculation in just a few clicks.
